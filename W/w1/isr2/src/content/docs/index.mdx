## Headline findings

- **The macro is expanding faster than the plumbing.** Islamic fintech transaction volume reached roughly **$198bn in 2024/25** and is projected to hit **$341bn by 2029** (11.5% CAGR), inside a total Islamic finance asset base above **$5.98tn** — yet MSMEs in developing OIC economies still face a **$5.7tn** financing deficit.
- **Nine of ten gaps share one root cause:** institutional liquidity exists but is structurally blocked from thin-file retail and SME borrowers, so the winning architectures are technology rails that sit *beside* licensed institutions rather than on top of their balance sheets.
- **Regulatory friction, not monetization, decides the sequence.** Malaysia, Bahrain and the UAE carry the lowest friction; Indonesia and Pakistan are the largest addressable markets but demand partner-licensed structures; cross-border stablecoin rails remain conditional.
- **The portfolio is deliberately barbelled:** four Tier-1 outliers (SanadFlow, HalalPort, QistHalal, SiratRemit), three capital-markets rails (JuzSukuk, AdlScore, TayyibLedger) and three defensive B2B SaaS assets (FiqhStack, AmanPayung, WaqfTrace) modelled at a **4.61x net MOIC / 27.8% net IRR** in a $50M seed fund construction.
- **The research itself was capital-cheap:** 4.65M billable tokens across 34 subagents for **$6.58** of direct compute at the model actually used, against **$35.08–$43.85** for the same workload on Western flagship reasoning models.
